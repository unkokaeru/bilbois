1. Customers 

An explanation of Faculty AI's existing customers and the customers they aim to reach. 

Faculty AI serves an extensive number of industries, as described on their website’s header under “Industries” [1], but we can see from the number of customers within each industry that their main industries are: 

“Financial Services”, specifically risk management, fraud detection, and customer analytics. Faculty AI serves three customers in the industry: Tide, a challenger credit bureau, and a private equity firm. 

“Health & Care”, specifically improving patient outcomes and operational efficiency. Faculty AI serves five customers in the industry: NHS and their AI Lab, Cera, Hywel Dda University Health Board, and a medical device company. 

“Energy”, specifically energy consumption optimisation, grid management improvement, and predictive maintenance enhancement. Faculty AI serves six customers in the industry: Northern Powergrid, Veolia, NESO FastOut/VoltaVisor, a gas distribution network, and Drift. 

This aligns with the common trend within the AI and data science community [2], since these industries often have significant data-driven needs and challenges that AI can address. For the sake of simplicity, we’ll focus on one customer from each of the three main industries and what Faculty AI have done for them; there’s a more verbose list in Appendix A. 

Tide (financial services) 

For Tide, the Head of Product is quoted saying that “Partnering with Faculty enabled us to quickly validate the idea of MemberSummarise. Together we scoped, built and iterated on a proof of concept to leverage their expertise in data science”, affirming their belief that Faculty AI could and did help. 

Specifically, Tide wanted to reduce overall customer service handling time by leveraging generative AI to both combine documentation with customer data and summarise customer data for human agents. 

This technology reduced overall customer service handling time by 10%, attributed heavily to their technology. 

NHS (health & care) 

For the NHS, the Chief Data & Analytics Officer is quoted saying that “By using this leading technology developed with Faculty, we are helping to support frontline staff in their ongoing mission to save as many lives as they can by equipping them with the most accurate information", demonstrating the critical role that Faculty AI played in the height of the pandemic. 

Specifically, Faculty AI developed the Early Warning System for operational users across the NHS to forecast hospital demand at multiple levels (local, regional, national), using positive case data, 111 calls, and mobility data from Apple/Google. 

This allowed the NHS to plan effectively to reduce strain on the healthcare system, relying on a system 112% better than a simpler model. 

Northen Powergrid (energy) 

For the Northern Powergrid, the Head of Forecasting is quoted saying “We chose Faculty because of their expertise implementing AI solutions for critical national infrastructure”. 

Whilst the quote is generic, it affirms their confidence in Faculty AI to solve the problems occurring within their business, specifically that the flexibility within their least-cost service has too much uncertainty for many asset owners to participate, since it’s the most flexible. 

Faculty AI helped by communicating a forecast and associated uncertainty, developed with historical load data, weather forecasts, as well as other factors, which has led to an automated market-based solution which is predicted to save up to £51m in operating costs by 2033. 
 

2. Business Model

2.1. Value Proposition 

Faculty AI offers three things: 

Frontier, a product (web application) described as “AI-powered simulation technology which improves the speed, quality and execution of decisions”. 

AI Services (delivered alongside an existing team), “End-to-end support for... AI”, split into six core services (strategy, design, infrastructure, development, operations, and change) and two specialist services (due diligence and safety). 

AI Talent (delivered in the style of a placement), a “Fellowship programme” of “pre-screened PhD and MSc graduates trained by the industry's leading AI experts”. 

Essentially, Frontier the web application offers an on-demand simulation to help teams make more informed decisions, AI Services integrate part of Faculty AI‘s team with a company to help them work with AI, and AI Talent reduces the cost of experimenting with AI, as well as reducing the follow-up hiring cost by offering academics transitioning into the practical world in the style of a placement. 

The two services aren’t comprehensively described, only suggesting that they’ll extend a “state of the art methodology” onto their customers. However, the product, Frontier, has a public white paper [3] detailing the technology, methodology, and approach. 

For details, the white paper itself is a very good resource, and a summary can also be found in the appendices (see Appendix B). Briefly, they use some technology called a Computational Twin Engine, which can provide simulations to optimise decisions, whilst maintaining governance, and then deploy these final decisions. 

Whilst not explicitly mentioned, this technology would require extensive computational resource, network infrastructure, human resources, and data resources.

Lastly, “AI Talent” would be a smaller revenue stream compared to the others, depending on demand, with the revenue coming from placement fees or ongoing contracts. The cost would be relatively minimal, apart from talent acquisition costs and possibly marketing/sales costs. 

All of these, however, are business-to-business, rather than direct to an end consumer. For instance, “Frontier” helps forecast to aid planning within a business, “AI Services” helps a business adopt and use AI, and “AI Talent” helps a business grow its employee population. 

Faculty AI doesn’t seem to publicly share if it has any recurring customers or partners, apart from those already mentioned, instead opting to work with customers to solve a particular problem or upgrade their systems to utilise AI and then move on. Appendix B: How does Frontier work? 

Frontier works using technology called “The Computational Twin Engine”, which combines AI, simulation, optimisation, and causal inference into a cohesive framework with four main capabilities: 

Simulation: 

Discrete-event simulator providing coherent (maintains set relationships), probabilistic (provides quantified uncertainties), causally accurate (maintains cause-and-effect relationships), and explainable outputs (for root cause analysis, using "Shapely values"). 

Generates multiple trajectories to simulate possible futures. 

Decision Optimisation: 

Compares decisions based on downstream business KPIs. 

Utilises Bayesian optimisation for finding optimal scenarios and tuning decision parameters. 

Decision Governance: 

Framework for setting operational bounds and monitoring compliance. 

Policies can influence or enforce decision outputs to prevent violations, by tracking metric values against defined thresholds. 

Decision Deployment: 

Integrates optimised decisions into operational systems. 

Supports both decision automation and augmentation. 

 You can design within the framework using structural objects (Elements, Processes, Stages, Decisions, Resources, and Metrics) and simulation objects (that govern event generation and state mutation during simulations).
